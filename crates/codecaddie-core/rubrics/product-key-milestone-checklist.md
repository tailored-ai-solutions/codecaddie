# Product key milestone checklist

CodeCaddie's product-readiness checklist, evolved from ThoughtfulBits product-planning practice; it is the written counterpart of the goal catalog.

## Product Strategy Overview

- The product should deliver results and outcomes (Results-as-a-Service) that get the customer's job done faster and more accurately than the alternatives.
- The product strategy should be:
  - Simple: anybody can explain it in a sentence or two without buzzwords. A successful strategy is one that thousands of people can repeat without the team in the room.
  - Known and Prioritized: the product solves a problem that customers already know they have and will prioritize solving.
- The strategy should clearly articulate the approach for:
  - Executors: the people who will actually use the solution. There may be several such profiles.
  - Beneficiaries: the executives whose organizations or business units benefit from the solution. There may be several such profiles.
  - Champions: during a sales process (even a self-service one), who likes the product first and sells it to the rest of their organization, and how does the product help them do that?
  - Ecosystem: what the ecosystem is and how the product plays in it (resellers, channels, VARs, ISVs, marketplaces, and similar).
  - Platform effect: how value grows as the number of customers or solution users grows (for example, how customer N+1 adds value for the first N).
- The strategy should play the curves, taking advantage of exogenous investments and trends (for example improving AI, serverless, and voice).

## Product Features / Roadmap Checklist

- There is a simple and clear way for the immediate user or executor to know the solution is solving their problems in a way that fits their workflows and expectations.
- There is a simple and clear way for champions and beneficiaries to see and share that the solution is solving their problems. Beneficiaries and purchase or renewal decision makers need to know that value has been added.
- As much as possible, the solution should be AI driven and automated, with the ability for humans to augment and correct as needed.
- AI-driven capabilities ship with evaluation tooling and runtime verification steps, and every automated action is explainable and correctable by a human.
- As much as possible, the solution should be proactive and learning, solving customer problems before the customer asks and improving its capabilities as usage increases (for example improving processing accuracy over time, or learning from a support representative's edits).
- The solution should have an AI-callable API and be integratable with the rest of the customer's IT and AI systems.
- The solution should have a "zero barrier" initial experience (free trial, forever-free version, or similar) where "solving the customer problem" is obvious as immediately as possible (in the first few minutes).
  - If needed, demo data should be generated or supplied to make this possible.
- The solution should make sharing easy: to new users (for example "invite a friend"), to colleagues, and to beneficiaries (for example "the board view").
- The solution should "self-market" new capabilities and upsell opportunities.
- Delightful experiences and "wow" moments need to be explicitly designed into the solution.
- The solution needs to be reliable and fast, working across devices (including mobile), with any long-running task explicitly showing progress and steps.

## Product Performance Metric Tracking

Goal: improve the product performance metrics each week.

- Activity and usage metrics: establish and agree on target metrics (nature and numbers). Examples: daily usage, number of colleague invites, number of "problems solved". Where sensible, activity metrics (for example product usage) should be cohort analyzed.
- Reliability metrics, on both server and client.

## Design Partner Plan Pre Launch

- Design partners (DPs) or an equivalent are in place.
- Launch go/no-go criteria are set: product performance metric hurdles for design partners (for example product usage or viral colleague invites).
- Feedback comes both directly from design partners, human to human, and automatically from the product (for example heatmaps and usage funnels).

## Customer Feedback Gathering

- The product team analyzes all customer calls (research calls, sales calls, and similar) and any other feedback (for example social media commentary) using AI to prioritize unmet customer needs, with readouts on an agreed cadence.

## Internal Operations

- There is a clear internal process for coordinating between sales, customer service and support, and product. Support teams are fully trained on new products and features.

## Go to Market

### Strategy

The go-to-market strategy should explicitly aim to bring potential customers, partners, analysts, press, and influencers "into orbit":

- Awareness: they get something of value from the company (newsletters, how-tos, a viral video, and similar), but their name and email are not yet known.
- "Entering orbit": the company gets to know them (for example they subscribed to a newsletter or followed on social media).
- Engaged: they are actively using something of value from the company (for example a free version, or data reports for analysts).
- Sales funnel: explicit motions exist for both self-service and sales-led paths from marketing into the sales funnel.

### Positioning and Pricing

- The team should know the product's core, clear value proposition to customers against existing offerings and competitors.
- Define the ICP for the launch: who are the targeted job beneficiaries and executors?
- Identify the top five competitors and alternative solutions users are searching for or mentioning in AI tools.
- For "do it yourself" competition, embrace and extend (for example, offer DIY templates).
- Create a "How Would You Beat" description for each competitor: how the new product beats each competitor and creates more customer value.
- Create the pricing page with free trial, credit card (if applicable), and enterprise pricing in place, describing the value delivered at the free, credit card, and enterprise levels.

### Metrics

- Determine the top two or three "most significant" metrics (for example the Facebook "7 friends" metric). There should be a most significant metric for each stage of the funnel:
  - Awareness / Visibility
  - Entering Orbit / Community
  - Engagement
  - Free version / Free Trial Usage
  - Activation / Conversion
- All other metrics should inform the significant metrics.

### GTM Team Execution

- The GTM team is coordinated with the product team in using AI to analyze all customer feedback, as above.
- Marketing content should:
  - be valuable to viewers (for example informative, helpful, or entertaining);
  - be authentic and human (real people, real voices).
- Use the latest AI tools to help (for example automatically creating multiple versions of a customer interview video, from long form to shorts).
- The GTM team posts new content in all channels daily.
- No spam of any kind.

### Community

- As much as possible, engage the community, customers and influencers alike, to share the product's message.
